Nellie is completing an M.S. in Computer Science at Georgia Tech with a specialization in Artificial Intelligence, expected December 2026. Her graduate work spans AI/ML, data, algorithms, HCI, and systems. Every course was project-based; the detail below describes what she built and measured in each one. A dedicated page on the portfolio lists all of it.

Knowledge-Based AI: symbolic AI, focused on agents that represent knowledge explicitly, reason over it, and can explain why they answered as they did. Covered knowledge representation (semantic networks, frames, scripts, production systems, formal logic), reasoning and problem solving (generate-and-test, means-ends analysis, problem reduction, planning, constraint propagation, configuration, diagnosis, and meta-reasoning under bounded rationality), and learning (case-based reasoning, incremental concept learning, version spaces, explanation-based and analogical reasoning, and learning by correcting mistakes). Agent mini-projects included a Sheep and Wolves river-crossing agent built as a production system with breadth-first control, optimal on all 20 cases; a Block World planner using pure means-ends analysis, valid on 20 of 20 and optimal on 16 of 20, running sub-millisecond at 26 blocks; and a Monster Classification agent that learned a version space from positive examples and near-miss negatives, scoring 19 of 20. The final project was an ARC-AGI agent: a knowledge-based solver for 96 abstraction-and-reasoning problems built on a fixed fingerprint-route-generate-test-select pipeline with about fifty grid operations across twenty-five rule families, accepting a rule only when it reproduced every training pair exactly, with no machine learning, no language-model prompting, and no per-problem branches. It scored 82 of 96 (85.4 percent), 42 of 48 visible and 40 of 48 hidden, at a mean of 3.6 milliseconds per problem across 99 passing unit tests.

Machine Learning: compared decision trees, AdaBoost, KNN, SVM, and neural networks on the UCI Fertility and Kaggle Airline Satisfaction datasets, reaching over 92% F1 with decision trees while diagnosing overfitting and class imbalance. Analyzed randomized optimization (randomized hill climbing, simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, MIMIC) including neural network weight optimization. Ran unsupervised learning with K-Means and expectation-maximization clustering plus PCA, ICA, random projection, and UMAP, evaluated with silhouette scores and mutual information. Compared policy iteration, value iteration, and Q-learning on Markov decision processes.

Deep Learning: built and tuned CNNs for CIFAR-10 with dropout, augmentation, and optimizer comparison. Implemented LSTM and GRU networks for text classification and language modeling with gradient clipping. Applied interpretability techniques including saliency maps, GradCAM, and guided backpropagation, plus adversarial examples and style transfer. Built attention-based seq2seq and Transformer models for machine translation, reducing validation perplexity from 19.1 to 4.9. The final project was Plate2Recipe, a multimodal system generating recipes from food images using Vision Transformers with a fine-tuned GPT-2 and LSTMs trained on RecipeNLG.

Natural Language Processing: built full text classification pipelines for IMDb sentiment and AG News topics, comparing Naive Bayes and logistic regression baselines against GloVe-embedding neural variants. Built a token-level RNN language model with perplexity tracking and greedy and temperature sampling, then upgraded it to LSTM with encoder-decoder attention. Trained CBOW and Skip-Gram embeddings from scratch and used GloVe for analogies and document retrieval. Built Key-Value Memory Networks for single-hop question answering over external key-value facts.

Reinforcement Learning: replicated Sutton's 1988 TD(lambda) Random Walk experiments under batch and online protocols, recovering the U-shaped RMSE curve across lambda. Built a PyTorch DQN for Lunar Lander with experience replay, target networks, and epsilon-decay exploration, passing the 200-point solved threshold. Implemented SARSA on FrozenLake and Q-learning on Taxi-v3. Solved planning problems with value iteration, derived the lambda-return, built a KWIK learner, and solved Rock-Paper-Scissors Nash equilibria as a linear program in CVXPY.

Network Science: analyzed social, biological, and infrastructure networks with NetworkX, validating spectral bounds, small-world effects, and power-law degree distributions. Implemented PageRank, eigenvector, and betweenness centrality and compared Louvain, greedy modularity, SBM, and HRG community detection, reaching over 0.9 NMI against ground truth. Modeled SIS epidemic spreading and identified epidemic thresholds. Estimated properties of large networks using capture-recapture and Horvitz-Thompson sampling inference.

Machine Learning for Trading: built a manual rule-based strategy and a bagged random-tree strategy learner on JPM stock from 2008 to 2011 using SMA, Bollinger Bands, and MACD indicators, benchmarked against buy-and-hold. The manual rules outperformed in some periods while the learner underperformed due to instability. Also conducted market impact sensitivity analysis.

Graduate Algorithms: dynamic programming, divide-and-conquer and the fast Fourier transform, randomized algorithms and RSA, graph algorithms including strongly connected components, minimum spanning trees and shortest paths, max-flow, linear programming, and NP-completeness and reductions.

Human-Computer Interaction: ran the full design lifecycle twice. The individual project designed a Smart Transcript Display for iMessage voice notes, aimed at users with hearing impairments and noisy environments, moving from survey-based needfinding through three prototypes to extended evaluation. The group project redesigned Discord's notification system using interviews, surveys, and heuristic evaluations, producing three prototypes (Smart Modes, Content Filters, PriorityHub) with PriorityHub selected. Her contributions included project ideation, survey questions and heuristic evaluations, user interviews, the Content Filters prototype, evaluation plans across phases, and the final survey analysis.

Graduate Introduction to Operating Systems: scaled a GETFILE client with a pthread boss-worker pool and a mutex and condition-variable work queue. Built a proxy-to-cache path over POSIX shared memory using shm_open and mmap with a turn-based producer/consumer handoff. Implemented a gRPC and protobuf file service with client-streaming Store, server-streaming Fetch, deadlines, write locks, and checksum and mtime based synchronization.

Computer Networks: designed custom Mininet topologies varying bandwidth, latency, and loss. Implemented a distributed spanning tree protocol and a distance-vector Bellman-Ford protocol handling negative edge weights and cycles. Built an SDN firewall with POX and OpenFlow validated with Mininet and Wireshark. Recreated BGP prefix-hijack attacks in Mininet and Quagga, and analyzed global routing data with PyBGPStream to measure prefix growth, AS-path evolution, and RTBH mitigation events.

Before Georgia Tech, her B.A. in Mathematics at William Paterson University (2016 to 2019, magna cum laude, Pi Mu Epsilon) included Applied Regression Analysis, Data Warehouse and Data Mining, Database Management Systems, Statistical Computing, Business Analytics, and Cloud Computing. That coursework fed directly into her undergraduate work as an ML Research Assistant, where she took behavioral survey data through EDA, cleaning, and feature engineering, modeled it with KNN, logistic regression, and hierarchical clustering, and presented the findings to faculty and student audiences.

Earlier still, she completed an Associate of Arts in Education (Grades 4-12) at Union County College in Cranford, New Jersey (2014 to 2016), with President's High Honors and membership in Phi Theta Kappa and Psi Beta, and a vocational Technician in Microcomputer Systems and Networks at I.E.S. San Juan in Seville, Spain (2009 to 2011), covering network operating systems, computer security, and network services, with an internship at CompuSpain doing hardware installation, system assembly, and troubleshooting.
